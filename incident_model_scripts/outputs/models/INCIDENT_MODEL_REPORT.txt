================================================================================
  FINAL MODEL SELECTION
================================================================================

  Global Hourly Incident Count : XGBoost
  Spatial 24h-Ahead Forecast   : Spatial LSTM
  Spatial Risk Surface / Map   : GWR

  Selection Criteria:
    1. All candidates evaluated via 3-fold walk-forward validation
    2. Models with MASE > 1.0 automatically rejected (worse than naive)
    3. Remaining models ranked by Poisson Deviance (primary), then MASE
    4. Best model selected on out-of-sample forecasting performance
    5. R2 used as supporting information only, NOT as primary basis

  Diagnosis Rule (Split R2):
    The training-phase R2 and the validation-phase R2 are computed
    separately and compared. Their difference (Gap) is the overfitting
    signal, and that comparison is the ONLY role R2 plays. Whether a
    model is any good is decided by MASE against the naive benchmark,
    never by an absolute R2 level.

    OVERFITTING   : Gap > 0.10
                    (training fit far exceeds validation fit)
    UNDERFITTING  : Gap <= 0.10 and MASE >= 1.0
                    (generalizes, but no better than naive)
    JUST RIGHT    : Gap <= 0.10 and MASE < 1.0
                    (generalizes AND beats the naive benchmark)

  Data Integrity:
    [OK] Walk-forward validation prevents future data leakage
    [OK] All lag/rolling features shifted >= 1 step (no same-hour leakage)
    [OK] MASE computed against naive benchmark (not as percentage)
    [OK] Poisson Deviance used as primary metric (correct for count data)
    [OK] Zero-incident hours retained as true zeros, not dropped
    [OK] Post-hoc incident attributes excluded to prevent leakage

  Note on the low Panel A R2 values:
    Hourly counts have variance/mean = 1.13, i.e. near-pure Poisson noise,
    so hour-to-hour variance is largely irreducible and R2 is capped near
    0.04 for ANY model on this target. This is exactly why R2 is not a
    selection basis here: it would reject every candidate regardless of
    forecasting skill. MASE ~0.84 shows all Panel A models beat the naive
    benchmark by ~16%, which is the meaningful result. Aggregating to the
    daily per-segment level (Panel B) averages out much of that noise and
    lifts R2 to 0.33 without changing the modelling approach.

  Not Included (incompatible with this KPI set):
    Logistic Regression, Ordinal Logistic and Cox PH are classification /
    survival models. They cannot produce MAE, RMSE, Poisson Deviance or R2;
    they require AUC-ROC, F1, Brier Score or concordance index instead.
